On the large Opalis planet, academies are unified. K-12 schools provide students with an opportunity to meet interplanetary students of many different ages and planets. Danny and Becky were local twins studying in third grade. 

On the lunch break, they were playing ‘Truth or Dare' with friends.
“Dare!” said Dan.
“Hmm, I dare you to skip your next class!” one of his friends replied. 
“That's an easy one,” Danny said with confidence.

When Dan’s and Becky’s class started Dan was still in the cafeteria. Becky usually liked to be near Danny, so they skipped a class together. After just ten minutes, boredom kicked in.
“Let’s hang out with the seniors?” asked Becky.
“Okay, the last one on the top floor loses!” replied Dan, rushing to the stairs.

When the twins reached the 12th floor, they entered an empty classroom to rest. As they were about to sit, a group of seniors went rushing into the classroom. Among them was Dan’s friend Matthew.
“What are you kids doing here? Skipping classes again?” Matthew asked.
“Yeah, but this time it was a dare! What are you doing here?” asked Dan.
“Just doing some work for my lab. Have you learned any cool stuff since the last time we saw each other?”
“Not really…” Becky tried to say, but Danny interrupted her.
“Actually, we just began learning mechanics.” Danny handed Matthew a book that was laying on the table nearby.
“Really? Don’t these classes only start in sixth grade? Says the same in the book.”
“Me and Becky are very smart!” lied Dan.
“Well, good luck with that then,” replied Matthew, who knew they only attend normal 3rd grade classes. “Don’t forget to take your book.”
“Let’s get out of here!” said Danny. Becky grabbed the book, and they went out of the classroom.

“What should we do now?” asked Becky while looking at the images in the book.
“I have no idea,” replied Danny, “but I have heard the school recently bought a new spaceship. Let’s go to the rooftop to check it out.” Exited, they rushed back to the stairs.
After reaching the rooftop door and ignoring the warning sign, they were on the roof of the school. There they saw a freshly bought spaceship. It was orange with shiny blue stripes. It was small compared to the ones in skyports, but looked magical under the clear sky. They climbed into the spaceship, fastened their seatbelts and imagined the ride of the spaceship. 
“I wonder if I can turn this on,” said Danny, pressing the start button on the spaceship. The engines hummed, and the lights turned on.
“Wow! This ship is much better with the light on," exclaimed Becky.
“Since we have a real spaceship right in front of us, let’s ride it for real, to show everyone we are actually smart,” suggested Danny. “Ready, set, go!” he shouted, pulling up the lever. “No turning back now!” The ship immediately shot right into the sky. 
“I can’t wait to see our planet from up there!” exclaimed Becky. “Just don’t press the wrong button.” The twins reached the clouds in under a minute.

“Wow! It’s so beautiful from above. Look how small our city is. It’s getting smaller and smaller with every moment!” said Danny, examining the Opalis planet.
“I think we are going too fast now. Can you slow it down?” Becky asked, concerned about their safety.
“Sure, I will pull the lever back,” Dan replied and pulled it, but the ship instantly spun around in all sorts of directions. They gripped onto their seats. Barely reaching the steering wheel, Danny managed to regain control of the ship. But both of them felt sick enough to rush to the toilet.

Once they felt better, Becky suggested reading the book she grabbed with her. The twins began to read, and it turns out the book was 400 pages long, full of words and terms they couldn’t yet understand. Besides that, the book wasn’t even about controls but rather about different parts of the ship. 

“I am scared, how do we get back home?” sobbed Becky.
“I don’t know. We can try pressing different buttons to see what they do,” advised Danny. Together they pressed a blue button. A noise screeched through the ship. The cooling vents shut down, and a strange noise came from the engines. The twins hugged each other, as the ship started to heat up incredibly fast. 
They sat down, fastened the seatbelts, and pressed every button in hope that it would help them. The cooling vents opened again, but the noise from the engines remained. Suddenly, loud sirens started screaming. 
“What’s happening! Are we going to die?” pleaded Becky.
“I hope not… I love you, Becky.” The warning sirens reached their peak. The red lights flickered rapidly. Then, an engine exploded, and the ship started moving toward an unknown planet. Both twins lost consciousness. When the ship was getting closer, the safety protocol activated, turning on the emergency braking system, until the ship landed on the planet.

By the time the twins regained consciousness, the ship was surrounded by rescue workers, journalists and even politicians. Some spoke of the twins' health, some built invasion theories, while others just reported the facts. The twins had a lot of injuries so they were transferred to the local hospital to recover. There they were told how lucky they were. During their uncontrolled flight, they had avoided nearby planets and asteroids. They also survived such a drastic crash, and launched the ship before it was disassembled for learning purposes. Their parents were held responsible for the damage caused to the planet and the ship. The twins learned that some ideas should be researched before acting on them.
