To survive quicksand, stay calm and avoid panicking. Instead, distribute your weight to the front, and lighten your load – let go of unnecessary items.

To tread water, maintain a vertical position, and lean slightly to make it easier, especially at first. Keep your chin lifted and breathe calmly. The goal of treading water is simply to keep your head above it.

During an earthquake, drop to your knees under a sturdy table or desk and hold on tightly until the shaking stops. Aftershocks may occur after the main earthquake.

If you ended up in the flood, go to the highest level, wear bright clothes to be visible and contact help, for rescuers to save you. 

To survive against an animal attacker, act big and loud to appear larger. Trust your instincts, and be aware of your surroundings. Learn first aid and practice self-defence to build confidence.

To survive against a human attacker, run if escape is possible. Leave your belongings behind and move away from danger. If you cannot evacuate, find an area out of the attacker's view. When your life is in imminent danger, fight back. 

After a catastrophe, prioritize your safety and health by checking on yourself, and getting immediate help if needed. Once it’s safe, manage long term recovery by seeking support.
